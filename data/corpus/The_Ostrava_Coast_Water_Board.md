# The Ostrava Coast Water Board

# Ostrava Coast Water Board: Comprehensive Reference Document

## Introduction

The Ostrava Coast Water Board (OCWB) is a critical administrative body responsible for water management and supply in the Ostrava Coast region of the Republic of Novomira. Established in 1963, OCWB oversees a network of reservoirs, treatment plants, tariffs, drought policies, and a range of environmental initiatives to ensure sustainable water use for the local population.

---

## Reservoirs

### Overview

The OCWB manages a total of **five major reservoirs**: Lake Vranov, Dam Nová Řeka, Přírodní Reservoir, Šumava Catchment, and the newly constructed Lake Zelená. These reservoirs collectively hold over **400 million cubic meters** of water and serve as the backbone of the region's water supply.

### Key Reservoirs

1. **Lake Vranov**
   - **Capacity:** 150 million cubic meters
   - **Commissioned:** 1975
   - **Usage:** Primarily for agricultural irrigation and recreational activities.
   
2. **Dam Nová Řeka**
   - **Capacity:** 120 million cubic meters
   - **Commissioned:** 1985
   - **Special Features:** Equipped with solar panels generating 1.5 MW of electricity, contributing to sustainable energy.

3. **Přírodní Reservoir**
   - **Capacity:** 75 million cubic meters
   - **Commissioned:** 1995
   - **Unique Aspect:** Designed as a natural filtration system to improve water quality in local streams.

4. **Šumava Catchment**
   - **Capacity:** 50 million cubic meters
   - **Commissioned:** 2005
   - **Function:** Acts as a flood control measure during heavy rains.

5. **Lake Zelená**
   - **Capacity:** 25 million cubic meters
   - **Commissioned:** 2023
   - **Innovative Features:** First reservoir in the region to utilize smart water management technology through IoT devices for real-time monitoring.

## Treatment Plants

### Overview

The OCWB operates three state-of-the-art water treatment plants (WTPs) that ensure the delivery of clean and potable water to over **500,000 residents** in the Ostrava Coast region.

### Main Treatment Plants

1. **Ostrava WTP**
   - **Operational Since:** 1970
   - **Capacity:** 100,000 cubic meters per day
   - **Technology:** Utilizes advanced membrane filtration and UV disinfection methods.

2. **Kralupy WTP**
   - **Operational Since:** 1988
   - **Capacity:** 75,000 cubic meters per day
   - **Recent Upgrades:** Completed in 2021 with a budget of 12 million Novomiran korunas, focusing on reducing chemical usage.

3. **Nová Ves WTP**
   - **Operational Since:** 2000
   - **Capacity:** 50,000 cubic meters per day
   - **Awards:** Received the Novomira Green Award in 2022 for its eco-friendly operations.

### Water Quality Standards

The OCWB adheres to the **Novomiran Water Quality Regulations (NWQR)**, ensuring that treated water meets rigorous standards. As of 2023, **99.5%** of water samples pass the stringent quality assessments mandated by the ministry.

## Tariffs

### Overview

Tariffs for water usage in the Ostrava Coast region are regulated by the OCWB and are adjusted semi-annually based on operational costs, environmental considerations, and inflation rates.

### Current Tariff Rates (2023)

- **Residential Tariff:** 50 Novomiran korunas per cubic meter
- **Commercial Tariff:** 75 Novomiran korunas per cubic meter
- **Industrial Tariff:** 100 Novomiran korunas per cubic meter

### Subsidies and Discounts

In response to economic challenges, the OCWB implemented a **Water Affordability Program** in 2022, extending subsidies to low-income households, reducing their monthly water bill by up to **30%**.

---

## Drought Policy

### Overview

The OCWB has implemented a proactive drought management policy to address increasing climate variability and water scarcity in the region. The **Drought Action Plan (DAP)** was officially adopted in 2019.

### Key Features of the Drought Action Plan

1. **Water Use Restrictions:** Enforced during periods of significant water shortages, reducing non-essential water usage by **40%**.
2. **Public Awareness Campaigns:** Launched "Save Water, Save Life" initiative in 2020, educating residents about water conservation methods; garnered over **100,000** social media interactions.
3. **Emergency Drought Fund:** Established with an initial allocation of **10 million Novomiran korunas** to support immediate water supply solutions during crises.
