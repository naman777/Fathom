# The Halvorsen Basin Water Authority: A Comprehensive Overview

The Halvorsen Basin Water Authority (HBWA) is a regional agency responsible for managing water resources within the Halvorsen Basin, established in 1967 through the Halvorsen Water Act. This document provides a detailed account of the Authority's dam infrastructure, water treatment operations, tariff structures, drought response policies, and key personnel shaping its governance.

## 1. Infrastructure and Dams

### 1.1 Overview of Dams

The HBWA oversees a series of five major dams situated along the Halvorsen River, essential for water storage, flood control, and hydroelectric power generation. The dams include:

- **Valley Crest Dam** (completed: 1978): Holds a capacity of 1.2 billion gallons, primarily for municipal and agricultural irrigation.
- **Silverbrook Dam** (completed: 1985): With a capacity of 800 million gallons, this dam plays a critical role in regulating river flow and supporting recreational activities.
- **Cedar Falls Dam** (completed: 1995): A newer addition with a capacity of 600 million gallons, Cedar Falls serves as a primary water source for the southern sectors of the basin.
- **Golden Ridge Dam** (completed: 2001): Designed primarily for flood control, this structure has a capacity of 1.5 billion gallons and hosts an annual hydroelectric output of 750 megawatt-hours.
- **Riverview Dam** (completed: 2010): The most recent addition, Riverview boasts a capacity of 1 billion gallons and is integrated with state-of-the-art flood monitoring systems.

### 1.2 Hydroelectric Power Generation

The HBWA's dams collectively produce approximately 1,500 megawatt-hours of electricity annually, significantly reducing the operational costs associated with water treatment and distribution. The Authority's strategic energy production aligns with the state’s renewable energy goals established in the 2019 Green Energy Act.

## 2. Water Treatment Facilities

### 2.1 Treatment Plant Overview

The HBWA operates three primary water treatment facilities, located at strategic points throughout the basin:

- **Northwater Treatment Facility** (commissioned: 1980)
- **Pinevale Treatment Plant** (commissioned: 1992)
- **Eastbank Water Purification Plant** (commissioned: 2005)

#### 2.1.1 Treatment Capacity

Combined, these facilities can treat up to 150 million gallons of water daily (MGD):

- **Northwater Treatment Facility**: 60 MGD
- **Pinevale Treatment Plant**: 50 MGD
- **Eastbank Water Purification Plant**: 40 MGD

Each facility uses advanced filtration and ultra-violet (UV) disinfection systems to ensure compliance with the Environmental Protection Agency’s (EPA) standards.

### 2.2 Innovations in Water Treatment

In 2022, the HBWA introduced a pioneering nanotechnology-based filtration system at the Eastbank Water Purification Plant, enhancing pathogen removal and reducing chemical usage by 30%. This investment amounted to $3.5 million and has improved overall water quality, garnering recognition in the National Water Quality Awards.

## 3. Tariff Structure

### 3.1 Overview of Tariffs

The HBWA implements a tiered tariff structure aimed at equitable distribution of costs among residential, agricultural, and industrial consumers. Tariff adjustments are reviewed bi-annually, with the last adjustment occurring in January 2023. 

#### 3.1.1 Current Tariffs

- **Residential Rate**: $3.50 per 1,000 gallons for the first 10,000 gallons; $5.00 per 1,000 gallons for amounts exceeding 10,000 gallons.
- **Agricultural Rate**: $2.75 per 1,000 gallons, reflecting efforts to support local farming communities.
- **Industrial Rate**: $4.00 per 1,000 gallons, with specific agreements available for high-volume users.

### 3.2 Future Projections

An anticipated tariff increase of 5% in July 2024 is proposed to cover the maintenance and upgrade costs associated with aging infrastructure and to finance new conservation projects aimed at increasing overall efficiency.

## 4. Drought Policy

### 4.1 Drought Management Strategy

The HBWA's Drought Management Strategy, first implemented in 2009, includes comprehensive measures to respond to water shortages. The latest iteration of this policy, revised in March 2023, introduces a tiered response approach structured around four drought stages:

- **Stage 1: Mild Drought**: A voluntary 10% reduction across municipal users.
- **Stage 2: Moderate Drought**: Mandatory water use restrictions, limiting outdoor irrigation to once per week.
- **Stage 3: Severe Drought**: Serious usage limitations enforced with fines, and assistance provided to affected agricultural sectors.
- **Stage 4: Critical Drought**: Water rations allocated based on essential needs only, with emergency relief measures instituted.

### 4.2 Community Engagement and Education

The HBWA engages the community through educational programs, workshops, and adaptive resource management initiatives aimed at promoting water conservation practices.
