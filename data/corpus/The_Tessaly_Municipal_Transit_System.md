# The Tessaly Municipal Transit System (TMTS)

The Tessaly Municipal Transit System (TMTS) serves as the backbone of public transportation in the City of Tessaly, a rapidly growing urban area with a population exceeding 750,000. This document outlines the various components of the TMTS, including its lines, ridership statistics, fares, ongoing construction projects, and governance structure.

## Transit Lines

The TMTS operates a comprehensive network of transit lines, which include:

### Light Rail Lines

1. **Blue Line**: Spanning 18 miles from the Northern District (Northgate Station) to the Southern Industrial Zone (Southgate Station), the Blue Line features 22 stations and serves approximately 40,000 daily riders.
2. **Green Line**: Covering a distance of 15 miles, this line connects the Eastside Residential Area (Pinewood Station) to Tessaly Central Park (Parkside Station). The Green Line has 18 stations and caters to around 30,000 daily users.

### Bus Services

In addition to light rail, the TMTS operates an extensive bus system consisting of:

- **Express Routes**: Primarily designed for commuters, these routes connect suburban areas with the city center. The most popular is Route 50 (Broadway Express), serving over 15,000 riders per day.
- **Local Routes**: The local bus network features over 50 routes throughout the city, with an average ridership of 100,000 per day collectively.

### Future Expansion Plans

TMTS has announced plans for the upcoming **Red Line**, which will connect the Downtown area (Central Station) to the newly developed Tessaly Tech Park. Construction is set to begin in 2025, with an estimated cost of $200 million.

## Ridership Numbers

Ridership numbers for the TMTS have shown a consistent growth trajectory over the past decade, particularly following the introduction of the Smart Transit Initiative in 2020. The annual ridership statistics are as follows:

- **2019**: 12 million total riders
- **2020**: 14 million total riders (14% increase)
- **2021**: 16 million total riders (14% increase)
- **2022**: 18 million total riders (12.5% increase)
- **2023**: 20 million total riders (11% increase)

Breakdowns indicate that approximately 60% of daily transit users rely on light rail services, while 40% opt for bus services.

## Fares and Discounts

Fares for the TMTS are structured to ensure affordability and accessibility for all residents. The current fare structure is as follows:

- **Single Ride**: $2.50 for adults, $1.25 for seniors (65+) and disabled passengers.
- **Daily Pass**: $5.00 for unlimited rides within a 24-hour period.
- **Monthly Pass**: $60.00 for adults, $30.00 for seniors and disabled passengers.

### Discount Programs

TMTS offers several programs aimed at increasing ridership among specific demographics:

1. **Student Discount Program**: Students enrolled in regional educational institutions receive a 50% discount on monthly passes.
2. **Low-Income Fare Program**: Low-income residents can apply for a reduced fare card, providing a 75% discount on all transit fares.

## Construction Projects

The TMTS has undertaken various construction and renovation projects to enhance the efficiency and accessibility of the system. Notable projects include:

### Central Station Redevelopment

Completed in 2022, the $80 million Central Station Redevelopment Initiative transformed the city's main transit hub. The project included:

- An expanded concourse
- Improved ADA accessibility features
- A new ticketing and information center

### Green Line Extension

In 2023, construction commenced on the Green Line Extension, which will add three new stations, increasing service to the southeastern suburbs. This project is expected to be completed by late 2026 at a cost of $50 million.

### Sustainability Initiatives

In line with the city’s sustainability goals, TMTS is converting its bus fleet to electric vehicles by 2030, with a target of having 40% of the fleet electrified by the end of 2025. This initiative is supported by a $30 million grant from the Green City Fund.

## Governance Structure

The TMTS is governed by the **Tessaly Transit Authority (TTA)**, which is an independent municipal authority established under municipal charter in 1995. The TTA is responsible for the planning, operation, and maintenance of the transit system. 

### Board of Directors

The TTA Board consists of 7 members, appointed by the Mayor of Tessaly, with staggered 4-year terms. Current board members include:

- **Chairperson**: Linda Valdez (Appointed 2021)
- **Vice-Chairperson**: Marshall Tran (Appointed 2020)
- **Board Member**: Elena Cruz (Appointed 2022)
- **Board Member**: Jacob King (Appointed 2019)
- **Board Member**: Tamara Ellis (Appointed 2023)
- **Board Member**: Omar Patel (Appointed 2023)
- **Board Member**: Rachel Soto (Appointed 2023)

### Policy and Oversight

The TTA is tasked with establishing transit policies, budget oversight, and ensuring compliance with federal and state transportation regulations. The authority’s operating budget for the fiscal year 2023 is approximately $120 million, sourced from fares, city allocations, and state funding.

## Conclusion

The Tessaly Municipal Transit System is an expansive and evolving network critical to the urban fabric of Tessaly. Continued investment in infrastructure, governance, and sustainability initiatives positions the TMTS for future challenges while promoting equitable access to transportation for all residents. As the system expands, it aims to not only meet the needs of a growing population but also set a standard for urban transit systems nationwide.
